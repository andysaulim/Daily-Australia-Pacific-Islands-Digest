China-Pacific

Solomon Islands scraps China security pact ahead of Australia treaty signing

Solomon Islands has informed Beijing it will terminate the 2022 security pact that let China rotate police training teams through the country, with about two dozen permanent Chinese police present at any time. Prime Minister Matthew Wale landed in Canberra on Tuesday ahead of Wednesday's signing of a new Australia-Solomon Islands Treaty covering security, health, education, justice, infrastructure and climate resilience. Former Australian high commissioner to the Solomons Lachlan Strahan said he was very happy to see the back of it.

AU-Foreign-Policy

Coalition rejects $500 million Vanuatu pact over net zero clause

The Coalition says it will not support the Nakamal Agreement with Vanuatu because it commits Australia to net zero emissions by 2050. The $500 million deal, signed by Anthony Albanese and Vanuatu Prime Minister Jotham Napat, underpins Canberra's strategy to keep China from gaining a military foothold in the Pacific. The government does not need parliamentary approval to ratify the pact, so the Coalition's opposition will not block it in the short term.

Wong reaffirms US alliance, warns US-China rivalry is destabilising world order

Foreign Minister Penny Wong used a major address to parliament to reaffirm Australia's alliance with the United States while calling for deeper partnerships with like-minded countries. She warned growing US-China rivalry is making the world more dangerous and urged Australia to move beyond nostalgia for a world that no longer exists. The speech came a day before Anthony Albanese finalises the Solomon Islands treaty and also flagged an Iran-linked AI operation targeting Australians.

AU-Defense

Parliament passes contentious defence security zones bill

New laws letting Defence Minister Richard Marles declare temporary defence security zones and appoint defence security officials passed parliament with Coalition support, despite criticism from independents, unions and legal experts over possible overreach. The laws respond to concerns that bases are under-protected against drone incursions, with penalties of up to seven years in prison for trespass at zones such as the Woomera Prohibited Area and Pine Gap. Independent Senator David Pocock called the powers dangerous.
