Pacific-Politics

Solomon Islands government 'dangles over the edge' as three ministers quit

Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale cut short his trip to Palau on Sunday after Home Affairs Minister Manasseh Maelanga filed a no-confidence motion accusing him of repeatedly making decisions on matters of national importance without properly consulting Cabinet. Three senior ministers, Maelanga, Justice Minister Clezy Rore and Commerce Minister Harry Kuma, resigned on Monday, confirmed to state broadcaster SIBC by the Governor-General's deputy private secretary Maxwell Banyo. Wale, 57, was elected on May 15 by twenty-six votes to twenty-two against Peter Shanel Agovaka.

So what: Wale's departure leaves Palau's Surangel Whipps Jr assuming the Forum chair without a handover from the sitting chair, and the motion decides whether the three-month-old GREAT coalition survives.

Pacific-Diplomacy

Five Forum members go without leaders as 55th PIF meeting opens in Koror

Five of the Forum's eighteen members, Kiribati, Solomon Islands, Samoa, Vanuatu and New Caledonia, are in Koror without head-of-government representation, RNZ's Teuila Fuatai reported from Palau. Kiribati President Taneti Maamau was reportedly unable to arrange travel in time and had a parliamentary sitting to attend. Nauru President David Adeang confirmed on Monday he would attend. Palau President Surangel Whipps Jr said fourteen Pacific leaders are still set to attend and the absences will not diminish the meeting.

So what: The secretariat is emphasising the robustness of this week's decisions with a fifth of the membership unrepresented at leader level.

Pattern: The Forum Foreign Ministers Meeting earlier this month failed to reach consensus on a statement condemning China's missile test, whose dummy warhead landed in Pacific waters near Tuvalu.

US-China-Pacific

US and Taiwan accuse China of weaponising travel advisory against Palau

The US Embassy in Koror denounced a Chinese consular travel warning, posted August 19 urging Chinese citizens to exercise caution in Palau over theft and robbery targeting tourists, calling it an attack and demanding Beijing "cease all its coercive activities against Palau". The embassy said the US advisory for Palau remains Level 1. Taiwan's embassy called the notice an "attempt to politicise and weaponise tourism". Palau's president's office shared the US statement without denying China's claims.

So what: The exchange lands as Palau takes the Forum chair and hosts leaders alongside Taiwan, which is attending after exclusion from the 2025 meeting in Solomon Islands.
