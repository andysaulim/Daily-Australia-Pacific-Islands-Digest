Canberra fast-tracks shoot-down powers against trespassing drones

The government is fast-tracking laws allowing the Department of Defence to shoot down drones that trespass over Australian military bases, the Australian Financial Review reported. Australian companies are simultaneously ramping up counter-drone technology aimed at protecting critical bases. The change would give Defence a legal basis to destroy uncrewed aircraft over its own estate, a power the current framework constrains.

Pacific-Diplomacy

Cocos Islanders denied COP31 seat as Canberra wraps Pacific pre-COP push

A delegation from the Cocos (Keeling) Islands, Australia's most isolated inhabited territory, will not attend COP31 in Türkiye after its application was approved by the United Nations but not selected by the federal government. The decision landed in the same week a contingent of federal ministers, including the prime minister, wrapped up pre-COP talks in Tuvalu and Fiji. The islands sit 8,900km from this week's Pacific meetings and face the same coastal erosion and flooding. The University of Western Australia's Oceans Institute said it remains committed to working with the community.

Burke demands Coalition cost its migration plan: "we are talking billions"

Burke demanded the Coalition publish costings for its deportation and migration plan, saying "we are talking billions," as the government opened a fresh line of attack on Angus Taylor's policy.
