Kiribati considers constitutional change to cement diplomatic recognition of China

Kiribati's parliament is set to vote on a bill amending the constitution to bar future governments from restoring diplomatic ties with Taiwan. The bill requires Kiribati to maintain relations only with UN members or permanent observers, which excludes Taiwan, and is backed by President Taneti Maamau. It does not name either China or Taiwan, and declares that ties with countries not recognised by the United Nations are "embarrassing". Kiribati has switched recognition three times, most recently in 2019.

So what: If passed, the amendment would end Kiribati's history of recognition switching days before Taiwan's expected attendance at the Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Koror.

Pattern: Kiribati last switched recognition from Taipei to Beijing in 2019, the third such switch in its diplomatic history.

Pacific-Politics

PNG parliament to finally address Bougainville decision

Papua New Guinea's parliament resumed Tuesday, with MPs set to debate from Thursday whether to ratify the 97.7 percent vote by Bougainvilleans for independence in the 2019 non-binding referendum. Under the 2001 Bougainville Peace Agreement, national MPs hold the final say. Prime Minister James Marape called the referendum "legitimate, peaceful and overwhelming in its result" and urged MPs to avoid division through "threats, fear, regionalism or hostility". Every member will speak.

So what: Parliament's vote, beginning Thursday, determines whether Bougainville proceeds toward independence under the agreement that ended its civil war.

Pattern: Bougainvilleans voted 97.7 percent for independence in the 2019 referendum held under the 2001 Bougainville Peace Agreement, which followed the province's 1990s civil war.
