US-China-Pacific

Australia and Palau press PIF for condemnation of China's missile test, Kiribati and Nauru resist

Australia and Palau pressed Pacific Islands Forum leaders at their September 3 retreat in Koror to formally condemn China's July missile test, in which a PLAN submarine fired a missile with a dummy warhead more than 7,000 kilometers, landing just outside Tuvalu's exclusive economic zone. Kiribati and Nauru again declined to back a joint statement, as they did at last month's PIF Foreign Ministers Meeting. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said he wants a "very strong" statement and rejected comparisons to US missile tests.

So what: The disagreement is being resolved during the forum's final day before the chair passes from Solomon Islands to Palau's Whipps Jr.

Pattern: Albanese first threatened a strong joint statement over the missile test in July and has repeated the threat without securing forum-wide agreement since, per SMH reporting.

US-Australia

Marles, Hegseth flag bigger US military presence on Australian soil

Defence Minister Richard Marles met US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth in Washington to discuss expanding US force posture in Australia, on top of existing Marine rotations through Darwin and the Submarine Rotational Force-West at HMAS Stirling. About 1,000 US Navy personnel are due at Stirling from next year to support Virginia-class submarine operations. Marles said further posture announcements, potentially including missile production, are expected in coming months.

So what: The expansion would land alongside decisions Marles and Foreign Minister Penny Wong are expected to unveil at their next meeting with US counterparts.

per ABC Politics: "US declares it will accelerate military presence in Australia" · also AFR: "Marles, Hegseth flag US military ramp-up on Australian soil"
Pacific-Politics

Solomon Islands PM Wale sacks finance minister as no-confidence vote looms

Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale sacked Finance Minister Gordon Darcy Lilo on Wednesday, hours after Lilo said he felt obliged to back a no-confidence motion led by fellow party member Manasseh Maelanga. The motion is expected to be tabled Monday, September 7. Three ministers, including Maelanga, have resigned this week, and Wale has elevated three backbench MPs to replace them, cutting the government's majority from 33 to 29 seats.

So what: The September 7 vote will test whether Wale's reduced 29-seat majority can survive further defections from his own coalition.
