Pacific-Politics

Fiji declares national HIV emergency as cases surge

Fiji has declared a national HIV emergency, with surging drug use driving an epidemic in which one in 60 adults is estimated to be living with the virus. The government has expanded free rapid testing and is giving clean needles to drug users, but the minister has ruled out mandatory testing. University of Canterbury senior lecturer Jalal Mohammed said Fiji has traditionally spent about 3 percent of GDP on health, against a World Health Organization recommendation of 5 percent, and now needs 6 to 7 percent.

US-China-Pacific

US confirms military presence talks at Cook Islands port

US Ambassador to the Cook Islands Jared Novelly said American military use of a planned port upgrade on Penrhyn Island is "all being negotiated", while insisting the project "is not a [US] military base". The United States and New Zealand are jointly funding the NZ$101 million upgrade at Omoka, near an airfield built by US troops in World War Two, with Washington contributing US$50 million and Wellington NZ$17.5 million. Cook Islands Prime Minister Mark Brown, New Zealand Foreign Minister Winston Peters, and US Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau announced the project on the margins of the Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Palau.

AU-Politics

Labor unveils migration overhaul, slashes backpacker numbers

Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke announced an overhaul of Australia's migration settings at the National Press Club, cutting the working holiday-maker cap for a second year to 45,000, down from the 57,000 who qualified this year, and for a third year to 5,000, down from 31,000. Extensions will be allocated by ballot and require regional work, with priority for migrants holding construction and agriculture skills. Farming and tourism leaders called the cuts a "low blow" for regional workforces.

Government scraps $5,000 allied health cap for veterans · ABC News
The Albanese government has dropped a plan to cap veterans' allied health services, including physiotherapy and dentistry, at $5,000 a year. Minister for Veterans' Affairs Matt Keogh said the threshold measure was "off the table" after consultation. The May budget showed the measure would have saved roughly $750 million over three years; it drew criticism from the Coalition and veteran advocacy groups. Canberra will also apologise to LGBTQ veterans.
