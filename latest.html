AUKUS

Crowd-funded inquiry led by Garrett and Barrie calls for AUKUS to be scrapped

A public inquiry into the AUKUS submarine pact, titled "Surrendering Australia: the Aukus Deception," calls the plan "the worst, riskiest and most costly defence decision ever made by an Australian government" and demands termination. Former Labor minister Peter Garrett led the crowd-funded inquiry; retired Chief of the Defence Force Chris Barrie served as a commissioner and warned Australia must not blindly follow the United States. The report puts the pact at $368 billion, with the first Virginia-class boat due in Australia in 2032.

PNG police identify Port Moresby wharf device as Chinese artillery round, contradicting embassy

Papua New Guinea police and military explosives experts identified a device found in a commercial shipping container at a major Port Moresby wharf as an undetonated 130mm Chinese Type 59 artillery round, high-level sources told the ABC, in a police report finalised today. The Chinese Embassy in Port Moresby posted on Tuesday that a company linked to the incident had conducted an "on-site verification" and found the object was "in fact a shell-shaped wine bottle." Four Chinese nationals were arrested on Friday.

Fiji military opposes coup immunity provisions in constitution bill

The Republic of Fiji Military Forces told a Special Parliamentary Committee it opposes new immunity provisions for coup makers in the government's Constitution (Amendment) Bill. Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka, who led the 1987 coup, told the same committee that Fiji relies on the military to maintain law and order during "disruptions" and has signalled possible intervention "if things don't go right." Commander Kalouniwai said the RFMF did not appear with "clean hands" and that past coups rested on an "architecture of rebellion" of secrecy, fear, personal loyalty, and constitutional immunity.
