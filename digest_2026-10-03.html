Pacific-Politics

Fiji legislates for its first referendum, one question only

Parliament passed the National Referendum Bill unanimously after midnight on Thursday, giving Fiji a legal framework for a referendum for the first time. Acting Attorney-General and Minister for Justice Siromi Turaga said voters would be asked a single question on whether they support the proposed Constitution, and that registered voters overseas may take part. The vote proceeds only if MPs pass the Constitution Amendment Bill 2026 by a two-thirds majority; the bill cleared its second reading 38 votes to five, with nine abstentions, and now sits with a joint select committee for 30 days of consultation. The Elections Office told the government last week it was ready to run both the referendum and the general election.

Fiji · Fiji Sun
Declared campaign gifts to villages exempted from Fiji bribery law
The Political Parties (Registration, Conduct, Funding and Disclosures) Amendment Bill 2026, passed on Thursday, provides that a gift declared in accordance with the law is not bribery under section 140 of the Electoral Act 2014, where it is given as protocol to obtain permission to enter a village or community group's territory. Declared gifts count toward campaign expenditure limits, and candidates must report donations and spending to the Registrar within 60 days of polling day. The reforms incorporate Multinational Observer Group recommendations.
