China-Pacific

Pacific Islands Forum closes with joint statement on China's missile test, Nauru alone dissents

On September 4, the final day of the 55th Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Koror, Palau, incoming chair Surangel Whipps Jr read a joint statement agreed by every member except Nauru, which "formally dissented." The statement calls on all states conducting intercontinental ballistic missile tests to provide advance notification "at least 24 hours in accordance with international practice," addressing China's July test that passed over several island nations. Beijing's foreign ministry said China is "willing to work with South Pacific countries" to safeguard regional peace.

So what: The 24-hour notification benchmark is now the standard Canberra, Wellington and Washington will invoke against any further Chinese test near Pacific waters.

Pattern: Pacific foreign ministers meeting on August 25 broke up without a joint statement on the same missile test; the leaders' text on September 4 carried over Nauru's dissent.

AU-Politics

One Nation takes safe Labor seat of Secret Harbour in Western Australia

One Nation's Luke Herdegen won the West Australian state seat of Secret Harbour, vacated by veteran Labor minister Paul Papalia, with Labor shedding 23 percent of its primary vote on a 19 percent swing. The ABC reported that internal Labor polling in the weeks before Papalia's resignation already showed One Nation on track to win. Notre Dame University politics professor Martin Drum said voters were frustrated with "the cost of living, housing pressures and their future prospects."

So what: The result hands One Nation its first breach of Labor's Western Australian heartland after a decade of state dominance and sets the terms of the party's federal positioning.

per ABC News: "How One Nation snatched the safe Labor seat of Secret Harbour" · also The Australian
AU-Defense

Stott Despoja to lead independent inquiry into ADF sexual violence

Former senator Natasha Stott Despoja will lead a one-year inquiry into sexual violence within the Australian Defence Force, a key recommendation of the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran Suicide, which found sexual misconduct was a "systemic issue" in the ADF. The inquiry covers prevention, reporting, and the ADF's response to the royal commission. Public hearings begin next year, and non-disclosure agreements struck between the ADF and victim-survivors will not be enforced for the inquiry's purposes.

So what: The non-disclosure waiver determines how much evidence reaches the public record before the inquiry reports in 2027.
