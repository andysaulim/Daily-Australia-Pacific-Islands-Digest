China-Pacific

Australia and Solomon Islands sign treaty barring foreign military bases

Australia and Solomon Islands signed a treaty in Canberra barring any foreign military or security base from Solomon Islands territory and committing Honiara to keep sensitive infrastructure free from militarisation, foreign interference and unauthorised access. Prime Ministers Anthony Albanese and Matthew Wale signed after Wale led a twenty-four-member delegation, including fifteen ministers, to the capital. The ABC reported Wale's government has told Beijing it will let the 2022 security pact, which allowed rotating Chinese police trainers, phase out at renewal in April.

Inflation hits three-year high, stagflation fears grip Canberra

Australian headline inflation reached 4.9 percent in the twelve months to October, the highest in three years and up from 4.3 percent in September. Treasurer Jim Chalmers called the figure unwelcome but expected, citing global pressures, and pushed back on the word stagflation. Reserve Bank research released alongside the data found even households untouched by rate rises are cutting back on essentials. Shadow Treasurer Tim Wilson said government spending was solely to blame.

AU-Politics

Digital Duty of Care law clears parliament, Big Tech faces billions in fines

Parliament passed the Digital Duty of Care Bill after Labor struck a deal with the Greens, exposing technology platforms to fines of up to 5 percent of global revenue for failing to prevent online harms. The Coalition opposed it, making this the first Australian online safety law to pass without bipartisan support. Shadow minister for industry Andrew Hastie called the legislation "heavy-handed censorship" and raised free speech concerns.

French forces seize 3.3 tonnes of meth from unflagged tanker near French Polynesia · RNZ Pacific
The French Pacific Command and French Armed Forces in Asia-Pacific and French Polynesia seized more than 3.3 tonnes of methamphetamine from an unflagged light oil tanker outside French Polynesia's maritime zone last Friday. Fusion centres in French Polynesia and New Caledonia had tracked the vessel for weeks with international partners; a US Coast Guard maritime patrol aircraft supported the boarding. The 150 barrels were taken to Tahiti for destruction and the crew released after an international report was filed.
