New Zealand says its ties with its Realm countries remain strong and stable

Prime Minister Christopher Luxon hosted the leaders of the Cook Islands, Niue and Tokelau in Auckland on Friday, the first of an annual series of 'family reunions' ahead of next week's Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Palau. Luxon said 140,000 Cook Islanders, Niueans and Tokelauans live in New Zealand. The meeting followed a frosty period after the Cook Islands signed a security deal with China in late 2024 that Wellington had not endorsed, a rift that led New Zealand to cut aid before restoring it earlier this year.

So what: The gathering sets New Zealand's coordinated Pacific position before Luxon and Foreign Minister Winston Peters travel to the 55th PIF Leaders Meeting opening in Koror on August 30.

Pattern: The Cook Islands' unapproved 2024 security pact with China prompted New Zealand to cut aid, a rift only recently repaired.

Pacific-Politics

PNG parliament decision on Bougainville still months off at least

Papua New Guinea's parliament adjourned Thursday after debating the tabled result of Bougainville's 2019 independence referendum, in which 97.7 percent voted for independence, without setting a date for a ratification vote. Opposition Leader James Nomane said MPs could not make an informed decision having only just received the Joint Consultation Report and Melanesian Framework documents. Prime Minister James Marape said his government had a plan for the way forward regardless of the outcome.

So what: PNG's parliament, not the 2019 referendum result itself, now holds the deciding vote on Bougainville's independence, with no date yet fixed.

Forum struggles with missile dilemma

Pacific leaders converging on Palau for the 55th Pacific Islands Forum want development partners to keep the focus on the Blue Pacific agenda of climate, oceans and human security, Islands Business reports, even as a missile dilemma looms over the meeting. The tension follows a Chinese missile landing near Tuvalu's exclusive economic zone in August, which left Forum foreign ministers without a joint statement.

So what: Whether the Koror meeting, opening August 30, can produce a unified leaders' communique will test the Forum's cohesion after ministers failed to agree one earlier in August.

Pattern: A Chinese missile landing near Tuvalu's EEZ left Pacific Islands Forum foreign ministers without a joint statement earlier in August.
