Leaked text reveals Australia's $980 million commitment to Solomon Islands

Australia and Solomon Islands have reached an in-principle agreement on a new treaty, according to a message from Australia's High Commissioner to Solomon Islands, Jeffrey Roach, to deputy prime minister Francis Sade, verified by ABC News and now circulating in political circles in Honiara. The message says Canberra has promised more than $980 million in additional funding to lock in the pact. Sources told the ABC negotiations have all but concluded, and Anthony Albanese and Matthew Wale could sign in coming weeks.

So what: The federal government is racing to sign the treaty in coming weeks now that Wale has warded off a leadership challenge.

Pacific-Politics

Solomon Islands PM Wale survives no-confidence challenge

Manasseh Maelanga, who lodged the motion of no confidence against Wale a week ago, withdrew it on Sunday afternoon and filed the formal documents with the National Parliament, saying the motion "was never about professional ambition, personal interest or creating division in our country." Wale, in office just over three months, had left the Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Palau within hours of arriving and returned to face the resignations of three cabinet ministers. He cited "a rising level of public anger in terms of how leaders behave in these situations."

So what: Matthew Knott reported the outcome as a blow to Beijing and a coup for Albanese, with Wale's next move shaping the Pacific's security architecture.

per RNZ Pacific: "No-confidence motion against Solomon Islands PM withdrawn" · also SMH Federal Politics (Matthew Knott): "The political survivor whose next move could decide the Pacific’s security future"
NZ-Politics

New Zealand's ruling coalition leads poll two months from election

New Zealand's governing coalition leads a new poll taken two months before the general election, Bloomberg reported. The vote is confirmed for Saturday November 7, with advance voting from October 26. The result lands after National used the weekend to promise an expanded low-deposit home loan scheme if returned, lifting the income threshold to $300,000 combined from caps of $95,000 for single buyers without dependents and $150,000 for others set in 2022.

So what: The lead sets the baseline for the coalition's campaign into the confirmed November 7 election date.
