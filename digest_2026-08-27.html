China-Pacific

Kiribati bill would bar any future recognition of Taiwan

Kiribati's parliament will vote on a bill amending the constitution to permit diplomatic relations only with states recognized as United Nations members or permanent observers. The bill, backed by President Taneti Maamau, does not name Taiwan or China but calls ties with unrecognized states "embarrassing" and like "a poor marriage." Taiwan lost the China seat at the UN in 1971.

So what: Passage would constitutionally foreclose future recognition switches to Taipei, three days before Forum leaders convene in Koror.

Pattern: Kiribati has switched recognition between Beijing and Taipei three times, most recently dropping Taipei for China in 2019.

Pacific-Politics

PNG parliament opens debate on Bougainville independence ratification

Papua New Guinea's parliament opened debate on ratifying the 2019 Bougainville independence referendum, in which 97.7 percent of voters backed independence. Ratification needs a three-quarters majority of MPs. Lowy Institute researcher Oliver Nobetau told ABC Pacific Beat a vote is unlikely this session, adding Bougainvilleans "are going to achieve independence by whatever means necessary."

So what: The debate determines whether ratification advances toward creating the Pacific's newest state, on territory holding about 2.7 percent of PNG's population.

per RNZ Pacific: "Long time coming: After decades of struggle, is Bougainville on the brink of statehood?" · also Reuters
AUKUS

WA hazard plan preps for 'one in 10 million' AUKUS reactor accident

Western Australian emergency services published a state hazard plan mapping the response to a radiation leak from a nuclear submarine or warship reactor, covering HMAS Stirling, the Henderson defence precinct, Fremantle port and Albany. The plan, prompted by increased submarine rotations under AUKUS, envisages iodine tablets, sheltering or evacuation in an "extremely low" likelihood scenario, coordinated by ARPANSA.

So what: The plan is the first published civil emergency framework tied directly to AUKUS submarine visits to Western Australia.

per ABC News: "Perth prepares for 'one in 10 million' AUKUS nuclear disaster"
AU-Politics

Malinauskas warns Albanese the GST deal will cost Labor east coast seats

South Australian Premier Peter Malinauskas warned Anthony Albanese he risks losing seats across the east coast if the GST carve-up among states remains unresolved. The warning follows a National Cabinet meeting at which Queensland and the Northern Territory secured carve-outs on energy for AI data centre development, while Western Australia's GST floor arrangement went untouched.

So what: Albanese has refused to reopen the WA GST deal this week, leaving the premiers' campaign running into the next federal budget cycle.

Pattern: Scott Morrison's 2018 deal guaranteed WA a floor of 75 cents per dollar of GST raised, at an estimated cost of $5.4 billion to the federal budget.
