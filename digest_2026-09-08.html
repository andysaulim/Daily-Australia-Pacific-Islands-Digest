US-China-Pacific

China reaffirms Cook Islands seabed backing as US scientist heads to Rarotonga

China's ambassador to the Cook Islands, Wang Xiaolong, reaffirmed Beijing's support for seabed mineral exploration through continued custom training programmes and ocean research surveys, RNZ Pacific reported. Asked whether China would also station a scientist in country, Wang told Cook Islands News that the US posting was largely for capacity building and that China had been running comparable exercises. NOAA's Dr Nicole Bartlett, a US Embassy Science Fellow, will be based in Rarotonga from November to advise the Seabed Minerals Authority.

So what: Both capitals will now have technical advisers alongside Cook Islands regulators as the country moves through its deep-sea mineral exploration phase.

Pacific-Politics

Fiji constitutional review report heads to Cabinet

The Constitutional Review Committee report on Fiji's 2013 Constitution, installed by the government of Frank Bainimarama, goes to Cabinet this week after more than six months of consultations, ABC reported. The report is not yet public but has already drawn attention over a Fijian Christian state and the role of chiefs in politics. Fijian Broadcasting Corporation reporter Mosese Raqio said the handover was an important step but that implementation would be harder: "These are not simply technical questions."

So what: Cabinet's handling of the draft constitution determines whether Fiji reopens its secular settlement before the next election cycle.
