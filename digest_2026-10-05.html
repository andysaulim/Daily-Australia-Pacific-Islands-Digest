Pacific-Politics

Fiji sets December 9 referendum, moves to suspend election campaigning

Sitiveni Rabuka named Wednesday, December 9 as the date for Fiji's first constitutional referendum and wrote to the Electoral Commission asking it to suspend all general election campaigning. Parliament passed the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026 on Friday, with only five MPs voting against; the bill now enters a 30-day public consultation before a third reading on November 2. Proposed changes include a Senate, a mixed electoral system, and the removal of immunity for those involved in coups. Rabuka said the general election must be held before February 5.

NZ-Defense

New Zealand declines Canada-led Defence, Security and Resilience Bank

Officials advised against joining the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank in late July, and the decision was communicated to Ottawa in late August, documents released under the Official Information Act show. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney invited Christopher Luxon in late April to negotiate the bank's charter and eventually buy in; MFAT flagged an initial $100 million contribution plus ongoing governance costs. Committed members include Ukraine and Turkey. New Zealand is separately lifting defence spending to 2 percent of GDP, or $12 billion, over eight years.

Pacific-Politics

Samoa's prime minister apologises for 2007 Nazi salute video

Laʻaulialemalietoa Leuatea Schmidt apologised on Monday after the Samoa Observer published a seven-second clip, filmed at a family reunion in Safotu, Savai'i in July 2007, showing him leading about 13 people in a raised-arm salute and the words "Heil Hitler." He said the words and gesture were "inappropriate and capable of causing offence and hurt" and that he rejects "Nazism, antisemitism, racism and all forms of political extremism." Schmidt was deputy speaker of parliament at the time and has led Samoa since the August 2025 election.

AU-Foreign-Policy

Australia's coal expansion draws Pacific fire as Pre-COP31 opens in Nadi

Pacific governments used the opening of the four-day Pre-COP31 meeting to challenge Australia, the lead negotiator and main funder of the talks, over its approval of a major coal mine expansion. Fiji's permanent secretary for environment and climate change Sivendra Michael said Australia "should be here to listen to the Pacific partners" and honour the 1.5-degree guardrail, adding that the New South Wales mine decision was a matter for Australian environmental law. In Nabavatu, declared unsafe in 2021, Marica Malumuca has lived in a tent for five years.
