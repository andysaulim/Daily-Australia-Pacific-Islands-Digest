NZ-Politics

New Zealand coalition falls one seat short, Opportunity Party holds balance

Final results confirmed Christopher Luxon's National-ACT-NZ First coalition lost two seats in the official count, leaving it tied with Labour, the Greens, Te Pati Maori and the Opportunity Party at 65 seats each in a 130-seat parliament swollen by a ten-seat overhang. Luxon said he would approach the Opportunity Party, led by a former KPMG consultant, to break the deadlock, calling the coming negotiations "challenging." Parliament must reconvene by January 14, though no deadline compels a government to be formed by then.

Pacific-Politics

PNG parliament to vote on Bougainville independence ratification December 2

Deputy Prime Minister John Rosso told parliament the ratification vote on the 2019 Bougainville independence referendum will take place next Wednesday, the final step in the 2001 peace agreement that ended the civil war. A three-quarters majority, 89 of 118 MPs, is required to ratify the result. Prime Minister James Marape said he was "burdened" by the decision, recalling a final visit from founding prime minister Sir Michael Somare before his death.

China-Pacific

Solomon Islands confirms security pact with China has lapsed

A Solomon Islands government delegation walked about 500 metres from government offices to the Chinese embassy in Honiara last month to tell staff the 2022 security pact with Beijing would lapse, following this week's treaty signing with Australia, the ABC reported. One analyst described the shift as Australia's "holy grail" in the Pacific. Chinese officials were informed under the pact's non-disclosure clause, and unconfirmed reports in Honiara say related papers were burned to avoid leaks.

Canberra sanctions two men over 2024 antisemitic firebombings

Australia publicly named and sanctioned Zohair Hassib, an Australian-Afghan dual citizen described as a senior IRGC agent now in Iran, and Kazem Hamad, an Iraqi citizen jailed for drug trafficking, over the 2024 arson attacks on Melbourne's Adass Israel Synagogue and Sydney's Lewis Continental Kitchen. The designations follow the 2025 expulsion of Iran's ambassador after ASIO found the IRGC directed both attacks, and come a day after an auditor-general report found sanctions enforcement relies on warning letters rarely followed up.
