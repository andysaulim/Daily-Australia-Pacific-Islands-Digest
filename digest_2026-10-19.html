Vanuatu expects China's police presence to fall after Nakamal Agreement with Australia

Vanuatu has flagged that China's police presence in the country is "expected to reduce over time" following the signing of the landmark Nakamal Agreement with Australia. Jotham Napat, Vanuatu's prime minister, says his government has yet to approve an increase in Australian police officers under the deal. China has kept a small police training team in Vanuatu since 2023, and its embassy has made substantial donations to the police force. Fiji's prime minister Sitiveni Rabuka made the only prior on-record commitment to wind back Beijing's police presence, in 2024.

AUKUS

Declassified details show Ghost Shark drones will carry sea mines and strike drones

Defence has declassified details showing Australia's Ghost Shark autonomous submarines will carry up to twelve sea mines at a time and launch swarms of Anduril Altius aerial drones carrying four kilograms of explosives each. Dozens of the vessels are being delivered to the Royal Australian Navy under a $1.7 billion contract signed last year. The Sydney Morning Herald put the broader expansion at $5.8 billion. The sea mine and drone capabilities are being offered to the UK and US under AUKUS technology-sharing arrangements.
