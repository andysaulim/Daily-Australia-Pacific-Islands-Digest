Peters brushes off Treasury on 2027 Forum hosting: 'Treasury can write what it likes'

Winston Peters said he had no obligation to take his offer to host the 2027 Pacific Islands Forum leaders meeting to Cabinet, telling RNZ Pacific he gave Prime Minister Christopher Luxon a mid-year heads-up and that "you don't know whether or not [PIF] are going to be taking up your offer." Treasury advised Finance Minister Nicola Willis in March to gut his NZ$30 million funding proposal, saying the value for money of the bid was not clear and that the absence of Cabinet approval made strategic alignment harder to assess. Peters said hosting rotates among members and it was New Zealand's turn: "Every Cabinet minister should understand that, even if they weren't told."

Former ambassador says Coalition MPs seemed 'almost delighted' by Beijing's coercion

Graham Fletcher, Australia's ambassador to China from 2019 to 2023, criticised the former Morrison government's handling of Beijing's coercion campaign, saying some in the Coalition "almost delighted" in being targeted and "paraded" the list of 14 grievances handed over by Chinese diplomats. Speaking on a podcast hosted by an Australian National University academic, he called China under Xi Jinping increasingly "smug" and closed to new ideas, and warned ideological fixations risk sapping the country's entrepreneurial drive. His posting spanned both the collapse of bilateral ties in 2020-21 and their stabilisation after Labor's 2022 election win.

NZ-Politics · RNZ Pacific
NZ First offers Pacific overstayers a two-year amnesty inside a tougher immigration package
New Zealand First will give Pacific visa overstayers 24 months to apply to remain legally, an allowance extended to no other nationality in recognition of what the party called its "unique and close bond" with the Pacific; those who fail to regularise would be deported and permanently ineligible for future visas. The wider package, unveiled by Winston Peters at Auckland's Immigration Conference, proposes a referendum on a 20,000 resident visa cap, stricter birthright citizenship rules, broader deportation liability, and a resident cap tied to funded infrastructure. Peters said immigration "is a privilege, not a right," and the party is demanding one of its MPs hold the immigration portfolio in the next government.
