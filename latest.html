Solomon Islands accuses Australia of interference over leaked treaty text

Former Solomon Islands prime minister Manasseh Sogavare said he fears Australia has meddled in his nation's internal politics after a leaked text message between the Australian High Commission and deputy prime minister Francis Sade surfaced. The Opposition said the message appeared to use Australian funding to influence a no-confidence motion against Prime Minister Matthew Wale, which was withdrawn just as Australia agreed in principle to a billion-dollar treaty package. Sade rejected the claims, calling the text an update on successful negotiations that should not be used as political ammunition.

So what: The row lands as Australia prepares to sign its next wide-ranging Pacific security treaty, testing Canberra's pitch as Solomon Islands' security partner of choice.

Pattern: Lowy Institute research fellow Oliver Nobetau said every broad bilateral agreement signed in the past 12 months has carried language on denying strategic access to third parties.

AU-Politics

Albanese unveils Digital Duty of Care, opt-out algorithm rules

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced draft Digital Duty of Care legislation that would let Australians opt out of social media algorithms in favour of a "following feed," building on the existing under-16 social media ban. Platforms that fail to reduce foreseeable harms would face fines of more than $100 million. Opposition Leader Angus Taylor and other Coalition figures raised censorship concerns, and Greens senator Sarah Hanson-Young said users must have "genuine choice" about content in their feeds.

So what: The bill would make Australia one of the most aggressive platform regulators among close US allies, arriving alongside higher tax bills for American tech companies.

per AFR: "Albanese announces new algorithm rules, shrugs off Trump" · also NYT: "Australian Social Media Users Could Get Power to Switch Off Algorithms"
AU-Foreign-Policy

Wong prepares new Israel sanctions, rules out trade ban

Foreign Minister Penny Wong is preparing new sanctions against Israel but has ruled out a trade ban, after Britain announced curbs on economic activity with Israeli settlements. Former Labor minister Ed Husic called the British plan a "seismic movement by one of our close allies." Israel's ambassador warned Canberra that broad sanctions on settlements would be "a deep mistake," The Australian reported.

So what: Canberra's calibration will determine whether Australia joins the settlement-goods import ban now adopted by Britain, France and Canada.
