Why Tonga's Chinese debt won't be forgiven, and who pays instead
Jack Salmon writes that while China has cancelled significant developing-country debt, that relief has almost entirely consisted of interest-free loans administered through the Ministry of Commerce and the China International Development Cooperation Agency, an instrument reserved for smaller projects. Tonga's larger commercial debt to China is not covered by this channel, and repayments are now peaking. Salmon argues Australia is quietly picking up the slack as Beijing shows no sign of forgiving the bigger loans.
Argument: China's Pacific debt relief has been limited mostly to small interest-free loans administered through commerce-ministry channels, not the larger commercial loans weighing on Tonga, leaving Canberra to effectively absorb the fiscal gap as Tonga's repayments to Beijing peak.
