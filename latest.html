Pacific-Politics

PNG parliament to vote in November on Bougainville independence

Papua New Guinea's national parliament will vote in November on the constitutional change arising from Bougainville's referendum, which recorded 97.7 percent for independence. Constitutional amendments ordinarily require a two-thirds majority, but the government says Bougainville independence requires three-quarters of members. Prime Minister James Marape addressed the Bougainville reports in parliament; member for Sinesine-Yongomugl and former attorney-general Kerenga Kua voiced the concerns many members hold, with some arguing the question should go to all PNG voters in a national referendum.

AUKUS

AUKUS partners run autonomy demonstration off Southern California

Maritime Big Play Pacific runs from October 5 to 26 off Southern California, testing more than 40 technologies from government and industry across the three AUKUS partners under Pillar 2. The Royal Navy leads the anti-submarine warfare element, bringing uncrewed surface vessels, underwater gliders, drones and subsea vehicles. Michael Dodd, assistant secretary of the Department of War for critical technologies, said the programme was assessing scenarios involving autonomous systems from "seafloor to space".

Pacific-Politics

Rabuka rejects claims constitutional amendments extend his term

Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka dismissed speculation that the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026 is aimed at prolonging his time in office, telling consultations on Rotuma the government is fulfilling an election promise to amend the 2013 Constitution. The bill needs a two-thirds majority at third reading before proceeding to a referendum; if that threshold is missed, the process ends. Rabuka said the work had to be completed within the term of the current parliament, which first sat on February 6, 2023.
