Trade-Economy

Chalmers books $15.7 billion deficit on a near-record tax take

Jim Chalmers released the final budget outcome for 2025-26 showing an underlying deficit of $15.7 billion, an improvement of $6 billion on the May forecast, driven by lower payments and higher receipts. The Treasurer blamed war and petrol prices for sticky inflation as the near-record tax haul drew scrutiny of Labor's economic management. The Australian reported the overshoot in receipts came in part from tax on workers' superannuation.

Record Greens support lifts odds of a centre-left New Zealand coalition

A new poll records the highest support the New Zealand Greens have registered, improving the chance of a centre-left coalition after the general election on November 7. The result cuts against Christopher Luxon's campaign framing, which bundles Labour, the Greens, Te Pati Maori, and Opportunity together as the authors of "nine new taxes", a claim RNZ notes reflects only Labour's own capital gains tax commitment.

Pacific-Diplomacy

Labor pushes to lift Pacific worker numbers as backpacker fight flares

The government is moving to boost the intake of Pacific workers, setting up a fight with One Nation's Barnaby Joyce, who argues backpackers should be quarantined from visa cuts because some Australians "are just bung lazy" and will not take the same jobs. Labour mobility is the largest single channel of Australian economic engagement with Pacific states, and the composition of the temporary visa program is now contested in both major parties.
