Vanuatu ferry sinking leaves more than thirty missing, MP faults search delay

The MV Matui sank in rough seas between Santo and Ambae on Friday morning carrying thirty-four passengers and eleven crew. Vanuatu's government says fifteen people are accounted for alive, one body has been recovered, and more than thirty remain missing. Luganville MP Matai Seremaiah said the vessel left Santo at 6am and capsized at about 9.30am, but boat searches did not begin until roughly 4pm on Saturday, more than thirty hours later, because authorities believed the ship was adrift and lacked coordinates.

Pacific-Diplomacy

Fiji ministry says it was not consulted on $1.5 billion Vuvale Union treaty

Public Works, Meteorological Services and Transport permanent secretary Paula Baleilevuka told the Standing Committee on Foreign Affairs and Defence he was not consulted before the Fiji-Australia Vuvale Union treaty was negotiated, though his ministry must implement major parts of the $1.5 billion, ten-year agreement. Consultation was run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Prime Minister's Office. The ministry does not know its share of that package or of a separate $1.7 billion one; the Water Authority of Fiji has proposed about $500 million in projects, including the Nadi-Lautoka water supply system.

Albanese confronts Hanson over attack on his late mother

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese became emotional defending his late mother, Maryanne Albanese, after resurfaced remarks in which One Nation leader Pauline Hanson called his references to a public housing upbringing "pathetic" and said it was "not on" for tenants to stay for decades.
