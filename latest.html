Pacific-Diplomacy

Australia and Solomon Islands sign sweeping security and development treaty

Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale and Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese signed a sweeping treaty in Canberra, with Wale declaring, "Those days are gone," referring to years of uncertainty over Honiara's foreign ties. The deal bars foreign military bases, covers ports, energy and telecoms infrastructure built under Chinese loans since 2019, and commits Canberra to A$740 million over a decade. Honiara's 2022 security pact with China lapses when it expires in April.

Liberal senator defects to One Nation in blow to Taylor's Coalition

South Australian senator Leah Blyth, a shadow assistant minister on Angus Taylor's frontbench, defected to One Nation on Wednesday, saying she did not believe the Coalition could effectively oppose the Albanese government. Blyth called One Nation the "only party with the bold ideas needed to turn this country around." Deputy Liberal leader Jane Hume said she "wasn't a big contributor."

AU-Defense

Labor-chaired inquiry warns new defence trespass powers raise human rights concerns

A Labor-chaired parliamentary inquiry has warned that new powers letting defence officials use lethal force against suspected trespassers in designated security zones could carry "significant" human rights implications. The committee flagged the absence of requirements guaranteeing detainees access to drinking water and humane conditions, and warned the law's broad reach could capture members of the public who stumble onto an undisclosed zone. Anthony Albanese dismissed the criticism as "nonsense."
