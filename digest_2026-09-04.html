Pacific summit backs concerns over China missile test despite Nauru dissent

Pacific Islands Forum leaders closed their 55th Leaders Meeting in Koror with a communique registering concern over China's July intercontinental ballistic missile test, which the text says overflew several members without adequate notice. Leaders called on all states conducting such tests to give at least 24 hours' advance notification. Palau President Surangel Whipps Jr, who read the statement, noted that Nauru had dissented, and Kiribati did not join the final leaders' retreat.

So what: The compromise language, folded into the communique rather than issued as a stand-alone statement, sets the baseline for how the Forum frames military transparency demands on Beijing going forward.

Pattern: An earlier attempt at a stand-alone statement on the test was blocked by Nauru and Kiribati at the Forum meeting reported on August 25, 2026.

Wale swears in three ministers ahead of no-confidence vote

Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale swore in three replacement ministers at Government House on Friday, an Office of the Prime Minister and Cabinet statement said. Nestor Ghiro replaces Franklyn Wasi as Minister for Agriculture and Livestock, and Harry Kuma returns to the Commerce, Industry, Labour and Immigration portfolio he resigned from earlier in the week. Wale faces a no-confidence motion next week as leader of the GREAT coalition government.

So what: The vote decides whether the Solomon Islands leader the Sydney Morning Herald calls one of Anthony Albanese's closest Pacific allies keeps office, at a moment Beijing would count as a win.

Pattern: Wale sacked Finance Minister Gordon Darcy Lilo on September 3, 2026 as the no-confidence push took shape.
